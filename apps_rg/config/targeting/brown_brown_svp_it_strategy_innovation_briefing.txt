Enterprise Technology and Strategic Innovation Briefing: Brown & Brown, Inc.
1. Executive Summary and Macro-Strategic Trajectory
Brown & Brown, Inc. (NYSE: BRO) currently operates at a profoundly critical technological and operational inflection point in its eighty-five-year history. Ranked as the fifth-largest insurance brokerage in the United States and the sixth-largest globally, the firm commands a market capitalization of approximately $23.31 billion and generated $5.9 billion in total revenues in 2025.1 Operating across more than 468 domestic and 246 international locations—spanning North America, Europe, Asia, and Bermuda—the company has historically built its formidable success on a fiercely decentralized operational model paired with an aggressive, highly disciplined merger and acquisition (M&A) engine.2 However, as the organization moves deeper into 2026, the macroeconomic landscape, the sheer scale of the enterprise, and the rapid evolution of artificial intelligence demand a fundamental architectural evolution in how enterprise technology is governed, deployed, and monetized.
The recent $9.83 billion acquisition of Accession Risk Management Group—which encompasses Risk Strategies and One80 Intermediaries—represents the largest transaction in the firm's history and has catapulted the company into a new tier of global scale.5 This single acquisition is projected to add approximately $1.7 billion in annual revenue, significantly accelerating the organization's push toward dominating the top five global broker rankings.5 Consequently, total revenues for the first quarter of 2026 surged by 35.4% to $1.9 billion, and net income rose 28.7% to $426 million.5 Despite this massive top-line growth driven by inorganic acquisition, organic revenue growth for the first quarter of 2026 was reported as entirely flat.5 This stark juxtaposition presents the central mandate for the incoming Senior Vice President (SVP) of IT Strategy & Innovation. Technology can no longer serve merely as a decentralized, back-office support function. Instead, it must function as the primary engine for unlocking organic growth, driving margin expansion, and institutionalizing operational efficiency across a newly acquired, highly fragmented corporate footprint.
The SVP of IT Strategy & Innovation is positioned as a visionary technology executive responsible for shaping the long-term technological direction of the enterprise while concurrently driving near-term incubation and agile deployment.8 Managing a compensation band of $300,000 to $325,000 annually, the SVP will report directly to the newly appointed Chief Information Technology Officer (CITO), Dorothea "Dori" Henderson, who assumed her role in February 2026.8 Together, they face the monumental challenge of rationalizing a heavily fragmented technological estate, unifying disparate data architectures into a cohesive data lakehouse, and deploying next-generation agentic artificial intelligence (AI) to capture market share.8 This comprehensive briefing deconstructs Brown & Brown's enterprise technology landscape, strategic imperatives, organizational culture, and market positioning, providing a highly nuanced, actionable framework for a candidate targeting the SVP, IT Strategy & Innovation role.
2. Deconstructing the Mandate: IT Strategy & Innovation
The job description for the Senior Vice President of IT Strategy & Innovation outlines a role that is inherently characterized by a strategic duality. The successful candidate must perfectly balance the rigorous, structural governance of Enterprise Architecture (EA) with the rapid, agile incubation of emerging technologies.8 The SVP must develop a comprehensive, multi-year IT strategy that is not merely a theoretical technology roadmap, but rather a commercial blueprint tightly aligned with the company's business vision, priorities, and revenue growth objectives.8
The primary pillars of the role demand a leader who is comfortable operating at both a 30,000-foot strategic altitude and a granular, ground-level execution tier.8 The foremost responsibility involves overseeing the Enterprise Architecture function, establishing cohesive architectural standards, frameworks, and policies across the organization.8 This EA oversight is critical because it dictates the establishment of architectural guidelines that promote interoperability, scalability, and security across hundreds of acquired agencies.8 Concurrently, the SVP is tasked with leading the enterprise innovation agenda.8 This requires a strong focus on AI, automation, data-driven capabilities, and next-generation digital experiences.8 The role demands the identification, evaluation, and incubation of emerging technologies—specifically in data platforms and integration ecosystems—to rapidly validate and prove commercial value.8 Furthermore, the SVP must build organizational capability, leading and mentoring a diverse team of technologists to elevate the firm's maturity in data engineering, modern technology practices, and AI deployment.8
A defining and persistent challenge of this role lies in the explicit mandate for "interoperability".8 Brown & Brown maintains a relentless M&A cadence, historically targeting 20 to 30 strategic acquisitions annually to broaden its specialty capabilities and geographic footprint.15 Each acquired entity, ranging from boutique technical firms to massive multi-national conglomerates, brings its own legacy technical debt, distinct Agency Management Systems (AMS), customer relationship management (CRM) platforms, and isolated data silos.4 The SVP must design an architecture that allows the business to maintain its decentralized, entrepreneurial sales culture—which keeps decision-making close to producers and customers—while simultaneously enforcing a centralized, rigorous IT and cybersecurity backbone.4 Achieving this interoperability requires deep expertise in modern integration patterns, API-led connectivity, and the deployment of unified data platforms that can seamlessly ingest, normalize, and distribute data across newly acquired operating companies without disrupting their critical local market momentum.8
3. Executive Leadership Matrix and Stakeholder Dynamics
To successfully navigate the organizational complexities of Brown & Brown, the incoming SVP of IT Strategy & Innovation must intimately understand the leadership matrix and the subtle power dynamics governing technology investments at the firm. The ability to build trusted relationships across business and technology functions, as well as the capacity to translate complex technology concepts into clear, compelling narratives for the Board of Directors, are explicitly required competencies.8
3.1 The Influence of the Chief Executive Officer
President and Chief Executive Officer J. Powell Brown serves as the ultimate architect of the firm's strategic vision and cultural ethos. He has publicly and repeatedly emphasized the firm's ongoing "technology and data journey," framing it as the foundational element that will dictate how the firm captures future opportunities in the insurance brokerage space.5 During the first-quarter 2026 earnings call, the CEO explicitly stated that the firm views AI not as a novelty, but as an enabler and an accelerator of their existing business strategy.5 Crucially, J. Powell Brown noted that for AI to be effective, it must be built upon "clean, standardized and scalable data platforms".5 This direct, public mandate from the CEO provides the ultimate justification and strategic cover for the SVP role. Before the firm can fully monetize the promises of generative or agentic AI, the SVP must execute a flawless data architecture strategy to clean and standardize the enterprise's massive, fragmented data repositories.
3.2 The New CITO: Dorothea "Dori" Henderson
The SVP will report directly to Dorothea "Dori" Henderson, who was appointed as the Chief Information Technology Officer in February 2026 to lead the company's enterprise technology strategy.10 Henderson's professional pedigree is highly instructive for any candidate applying for this role. Prior to joining Brown & Brown, she spent 18 years at Collins Aerospace, a Raytheon Technologies company, holding executive leadership roles in cloud engineering, cybersecurity, global networks, and mission-critical operations.10 Recognizing her influence, she was named a 2025 Modern Healthcare Leading Women honoree, highlighting her capability in shaping technology-driven healthcare solutions.10 During the COVID-19 pandemic at Collins Aerospace, Henderson demonstrated a bias for rapid, critical-path innovation under extreme pressure, quickly deploying 24/7 helpdesks and developing new iPad app overlays to help pilots navigate complex global travel restrictions.20
Her appointment signals a profound shift in how Brown & Brown views enterprise technology. By recruiting an executive from the defense and aerospace sector—industries characterized by an absolute zero-tolerance for error, massive global scale, and the highest tiers of cybersecurity protocols—the firm is clearly signaling a departure from legacy, localized insurance IT models.10 Henderson's stated mandate includes driving a companywide data and artificial intelligence strategy, strengthening system reliability, supporting technology integration for acquisitions, and accelerating digital capabilities.11 The SVP of IT Strategy & Innovation must align perfectly with Henderson's preference for rigorous engineering standards, resilient technology ecosystems, and structured digital transformation, translating her high-level C-suite vision into actionable, multi-year architectural roadmaps.

	4. Financial Imperatives and the M&A Integration Playbook
The mandate for the SVP of IT Strategy & Innovation cannot be fully comprehended without a deep analysis of Brown & Brown's financial structure and its aggressive M&A playbook. In 2025, the company generated $5.9 billion in total revenues, a 22.8% increase over 2024, with net income rising to $1.05 billion.3 The business operates across 468 domestic locations and 246 international locations, generating substantial revenue from global operations in the UK, Ireland, Canada, and beyond.3
The defining financial event shaping the current IT strategy is the $9.83 billion acquisition of Accession Risk Management Group, completed in August 2025.5 Accession, the parent company of retail brokerage Risk Strategies and wholesaler One80 Intermediaries, was previously the 13th-largest broker of U.S. business.5 Risk Strategies brought a consultancy heritage with embedded client relationships focused on middle-market corporate clients and high-net-worth individuals, while One80 Intermediaries operated over 100 specialized programs.27 This acquisition is expected to add approximately $1.7 billion in annual revenue, fundamentally altering the scale of the enterprise.5 Furthermore, the firm has consistently executed numerous other strategic acquisitions, including GRP in the UK, Orchid Underwriters Agency, BdB Limited, NBS Insurance Agency (Local Edge), and assets from Poulton Associates.27
This relentless acquisition cadence creates a massive challenge for the IT organization. Historically, Brown & Brown operates with a decentralized management style, allowing acquired firms to retain local leadership and market identity.4 While corporate functions handle public-company reporting and treasury, the customer-facing operating model is intentionally local.4 However, from an Enterprise Architecture perspective, unchecked decentralization leads to an unsustainable accumulation of technical debt, severe cybersecurity vulnerabilities, and an absolute inability to leverage aggregate enterprise data.16
The SVP must design an integration playbook that resolves this tension. Centralized IT functions must handle robust cybersecurity controls, unified data lakes, cloud infrastructure, and compliance enforcement, while decentralized applications and front-end interfaces must be allowed to persist if they serve highly specialized local market needs.4 The cost and complexity of integrating information technology systems post-acquisition are closely monitored by investors as nonrecurring acquisition costs.27 The SVP must therefore pioneer frameworks that accelerate the technical integration of acquired entities, utilizing modern integration patterns and APIs to rapidly connect acquired data silos to the central Brown & Brown data warehouse, ensuring that the firm can quickly recognize revenue synergies and eliminate redundant software licensing costs without crushing the entrepreneurial spirit of the acquired agencies.8
5. Enterprise Architecture and Multi-Cloud Modernization
The foundation of the SVP role rests on overseeing the Enterprise Architecture function, establishing guidelines that promote scalability, security, and modernization.8 This requires moving the organization from a highly fragmented, post-acquisition state to a cohesive, agile, and secure future state capable of supporting advanced AI workloads.
To govern this complexity, the SVP must likely rely on established enterprise architecture frameworks such as TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework) or the Zachman Framework.29 These methodologies provide the strategic blueprints required to align business objectives with IT infrastructure, establishing strict, repeatable guidelines for cloud deployment, data ingestion, and system rationalization. The rapid rise of multi-cloud and hybrid-cloud environments as default architectures in the enterprise space requires the SVP to design systems that avoid vendor lock-in while optimizing performance, cost predictability, and AI capabilities.31
The insurance sector is witnessing a massive transition toward cloud-native infrastructures.5 Given the global scale of Brown & Brown, a sophisticated multi-cloud strategy is almost certainly required, intelligently balancing workloads across Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform (GCP).32 The SVP will guide critical decisions regarding workload placement and cloud interoperability. For instance, the firm may heavily leverage Microsoft Azure for deep integration with existing Microsoft 365 environments, enterprise Active Directory security, and platforms like Azure Synapse Analytics.35 Concurrently, AWS may be utilized for high-performance computing, serverless event-driven architectures (such as AWS Lambda and Step Functions processing millions of daily transactions), and scalable, resilient storage via Amazon S3.36 Furthermore, Google Cloud's recent unprecedented $750 million investment in agentic AI ecosystems and partner incentives suggests that GCP may play a pivotal role in the firm's advanced AI incubation and machine learning efforts.33 The SVP must ensure that these cloud environments are not isolated islands, but rather highly connected ecosystems managed through robust Cloud Center of Excellence (CCoE) principles.
6. Unified Data Platforms and Modern Data Engineering
As explicitly stated by CEO J. Powell Brown, artificial intelligence is entirely dependent upon clean, standardized, and scalable data platforms.5 The current technological architecture across the newly expanded Brown & Brown footprint inevitably suffers from deeply siloed data trapped within disparate legacy AMS systems, disjointed billing platforms, and isolated CRM instances across hundreds of acquired agencies.12
To satisfy the JD's requirement for "deep expertise in data platforms," the technical roadmap must prioritize the deployment of a unified data platform, often referred to as a data lake or data lakehouse.8 Architectures utilizing platforms such as Snowflake, Databricks, or Cisco Data Intelligence Platform (CDIP) are critical in this arena.29 These platforms allow for the massive ingestion of both structured data (financials, policy limits, claims data) and unstructured data (PDF policies, broker emails, medical records) into a central, highly governed repository. Once centralized, this data can be normalized, transformed, and securely accessed by advanced machine learning models and business intelligence tools like Power BI or Tableau.12
Building organizational capability in modern data engineering is explicitly listed as a core deliverable for the SVP.8 This requires recruiting, retaining, and upskilling talent proficient in Python, SQL, Apache Spark, Hadoop, and modern ETL/ELT (Extract, Transform, Load / Extract, Load, Transform) pipeline design.29 By dismantling data silos and establishing a single source of truth, the organization can finally achieve a 360-degree, holistic view of the customer. This unified perspective enables predictive cross-selling across the Retail and Specialty Distribution segments, facilitates superior actuarial risk profiling, and provides the firm with an impenetrable negotiating position when brokering capacity with global carrier partners.12

8. Artificial Intelligence and the Innovation Incubation Agenda
The most forward-looking, visionary aspect of the SVP role involves leading the enterprise innovation agenda, establishing formal innovation programs, labs, and pilots to rapidly test and prove the commercial value of emerging concepts.8 The global insurance industry is rapidly transitioning its AI investments away from simple robotic process automation (RPA) and basic chatbots toward "smart tools" designed for complex risk evaluation, autonomous workflows, and advanced underwriting.5
8.1 Architecting the Innovation Lab and Agile Incubation
The SVP is tasked with creating an environment that fosters a culture of experimentation, continuous learning, and responsible innovation.8 This "Innovation Lab" or "Center of Excellence" must be structured to run agile, iterative incubation efforts rather than engaging in multi-year, monolithic, and inflexible software development life cycles.8 The lab must rapidly test, validate, and scale minimum viable products (MVPs) that deliver immediate, measurable value.8
Crucially, this innovation cannot happen in an IT vacuum. As noted by Specialty Distribution CEO Steve Boyd, the firm explicitly prefers "bottom-up use cases from the business" rather than top-down, theoretical IT mandates.23 The SVP must act as a strategic liaison, embedding technologists deeply with business unit leaders to identify operational friction points and rapidly co-create AI-enabled strategies that drive growth and transformation.8
8.2 Operationalizing AI: High-Value Commercial Use Cases
Analytical evaluation of the firm's operations reveals several highly lucrative avenues for AI monetization that the SVP should champion:
* Automated Policy Checking and Error Reduction: Utilizing Natural Language Processing (NLP) and specialized Large Language Models (LLMs) to ingest and read hundreds of pages of dense, complex insurance contracts. These models can instantly identify coverage gaps, compare policy limits against historical benchmarking data, and detect errors and omissions.45 This drastically reduces the firm's E&O liability exposure while dramatically increasing the volume of policies a broker can process.
* Data Extraction and Financial Reconciliation: Deploying advanced computer vision and AI-driven optical character recognition (OCR) to extract unstructured data from highly variable carrier documents, seamlessly integrating this data into the firm's financial and ERP systems to streamline the revenue recognition process and automate direct billing.45
* Predictive Analytics in Catastrophe Risk: Utilizing machine learning algorithms to ingest real-time climate data, telemetry, and geopolitical risk factors to provide superior, data-backed risk advisory services to commercial clients facing hardening property markets and massive catastrophe exposures.26
* Agentic AI and Autonomous Workflows: Moving beyond reactive chat interfaces to proactive, autonomous "Agentic AI." Platforms that can independently monitor business signals across the digital ecosystem (e.g., detecting a client opening a new facility via public filings, or a regulatory change in a specific state) and automatically trigger multi-step workflows—such as generating a coverage review brief or scheduling a broker follow-up—without requiring human initiation.37
8.3 Navigating AI Risk, Governance, and Security
The deployment of artificial intelligence in a highly regulated, data-sensitive industry like insurance carries profound enterprise risks. The SVP must be the ultimate champion of "responsible innovation".8 Brown & Brown has actively published thought leadership on the severe exposures created by unchecked AI.56
The SVP must implement rigorous architectural guardrails to mitigate the risk of AI hallucinations, where LLMs fabricate information, cite nonexistent case law, or invent inaccurate risk parameters.56 This requires the implementation of strict human-in-the-loop (HITL) validation frameworks and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) architectures, which restrict the AI to querying only verified, proprietary corporate data rather than the open internet. Furthermore, there is a severe organizational risk of "Shadow AI"—employees inputting confidential client data, protected health information (PHI), or proprietary underwriting formulas into public AI models, resulting in catastrophic data breaches and the loss of intellectual property.56 The innovation lab must provide highly secure, internal, ring-fenced AI environments and enforce strict enterprise-wide governance policies regarding acceptable AI use.
9. Strategic Market Dynamics and Human Capital Defense
An effective, resilient IT strategy cannot be developed in an academic vacuum; it must be deeply responsive to the prevailing macroeconomic environment, evolving market dynamics, and internal corporate challenges.
9.1 The 2026 Market Trends Context
Brown & Brown's 2026 Market Trends Report highlights that while overall market capacity has increased and commercial rates are softening in some areas, the environment remains highly volatile and nuanced.7 Carrier competition currently favors "well-prepared buyers with positive claims histories," while sectors such as transportation and hospitality, alongside catastrophe-prone regions, face restrictive terms and elevated pricing.7
The SVP of IT Strategy & Innovation must ensure that the technology stack directly addresses these market realities. By providing brokers with advanced data analytics, predictive modeling, and highly visualized risk dashboards, Brown & Brown can empower its clients to present themselves as optimal, "well-prepared buyers" to the carrier market, securing superior rates, enhancing client loyalty, and differentiating the firm from technologically lagging competitors.26
9.2 Defending Against Human Capital Flight via Technology
A highly sensitive but critical backdrop to the current corporate environment is the ongoing legal and operational conflict with competitor Howden U.S..5 In late 2025 and early 2026, Brown & Brown filed lawsuits alleging an aggressive "employee raid," resulting in the loss of approximately 200 staff members in Massachusetts, with Howden recruiting roughly 700 staff for its U.S. retail operations since August 2025.5
While fundamentally a legal and human resources issue, this mass poaching event carries profound technological implications. When an insurance brokerage loses a substantial amount of localized institutional knowledge and relationship history overnight, the severe vulnerability of relying solely on human expertise is dangerously exposed. The SVP of IT Strategy & Innovation can and should leverage this crisis as a mandate to accelerate the deployment of institutional knowledge capture systems. By deploying AI to document broker workflows, centralize unstructured communications into searchable knowledge bases, and automate routine client touchpoints, the firm can build a resilient, "sticky" digital infrastructure. This ensures that if a team departs, the relationship history, pricing data, and operational context remain seamlessly retained within Brown & Brown's secure CRM environments, mitigating the operational impact of future human capital flight.5
10. Cultural Alignment: Translating "The Power of We" into Tech Leadership
Brown & Brown possesses a fiercely defended, highly distinct corporate culture. A candidate who possesses brilliant technical acumen but fails to weave this cultural vocabulary into their strategic vision will inherently fail to secure or execute the role.
The company describes itself as a strict meritocracy governed by the principle: "Work Hard. Get Rewarded".8 It operates under the philosophy of "The Power of We," emphasizing teamwork, trust, and courage.58 The firm explicitly rejects traditional corporate hierarchy language, referring to staff exclusively as "teammates" rather than "employees," and "leaders" rather than "managers".58 For the SVP of IT Strategy & Innovation, this dictates a collaborative leadership style. The IT department cannot operate as an arrogant, isolated entity dictating terms to the business. IT strategy must be co-created in deep, trusted partnership with local profit center leaders.8 The technology deployed must genuinely empower teammates to thrive, enhancing their uncapped compensation potential by removing administrative friction and allowing them to focus entirely on customer relationships.58
Since the 1980s, the Cheetah has served as the enduring symbol of Brown & Brown's culture, representing vision, swiftness, strength, and agility.58 This is an ideal, culturally resonant metaphor for the Innovation Lab. The SVP should frame their incubation methodology around the "Cheetah mentality"—agile, exceptionally fast, highly targeted, and ruthlessly effective in execution. Furthermore, nearly 20% of the company is owned by its teammates through the Teammate Stock Purchase Plan (TSPP).58 This ownership mindset is a critical lever for the SVP. When proposing expensive, multi-year IT overhauls, the SVP must justify the return on investment (ROI) not just to the executive board, but with the profound understanding that the end-users of the technology are literal shareholders whose personal financial wealth is intimately tied to the firm's margin expansion, technological efficiency, and stock performance.6
Finally, the SVP must leverage the firm's Teammate Resource Groups (TRGs)—which support Veterans, Mental Health, and individuals of all abilities—to champion diversity, inclusion, and belonging, actively building a diverse team of technologists and architects that reflects the communities they serve.8
