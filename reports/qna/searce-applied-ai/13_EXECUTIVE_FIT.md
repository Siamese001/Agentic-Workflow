## LIVE VERBAL-FIRST OVERWRITE

These cards are optimized for live interview readout.

### Always-on output rules
- Use **short clauses** and a natural spoken cadence.
- Prefer **bullets** for architecture, governance, STAR, RCA, and technical answers.
- Limit live answers to **4 to 5 top-level bullets** unless Amit explicitly asks for depth.
- Use sub-bullets only when they make the answer easier to read under pressure.
- Use **bold** for words that should carry weight.
- Use *italics* for softer emphasis, contrast, or pacing.
- Do not over-format.
- Avoid forced summary endings such as polished "that is the difference between" lines.
- End on the practical implication or final control point.

### First-person credibility
Use lightly and naturally:
- "What I have seen in my agentic work..."
- "What I have learned building agentic workflows..."
- "My bias is to design the failure mode first..."

### Tight routing rule
- Architecture stays architecture-first.
- Governance stays risk/control-first.
- Do not drift into STAR, DGS, ROI, or 90-day plan unless asked.

### Inline citation discipline
- Every claim about Searce that came from research must carry a `[S#]` tag resolved in card 19 (Source Register).
- Personal experience (STAR, RCA) needs no citation.
- If you cannot cite a research claim, downgrade the framing to "my read is" or drop it.

### Preferred reliability-chain phrasing
When a reliability chain is useful, use these clusters instead of a long control inventory:
- **trusted data and semantic grounding**
- **validated execution and model lifecycle**
- **governed action and policy gates**
- **observability, audit, and regression evals**

## Searce-specific close patterns
- "I am a DRI by instinct — at Unify I owned the platform roadmap, the architecture, the engineering scale-out (8→28), AND the commercialization ($22M productized revenue, 20% margin expansion). That's the DRI shape Searce describes; it's the shape I've actually executed. I will not need you in every deal."
- "My job is to make your motion bigger, not compete with it. The line I'd draw with you: you and Siddharth own the broader Americas commercial relationship; I own the Applied AI pipeline within it — pursuit shaping, technical credibility at CxO level, delivery accountability, IP capture. We meet at quarterly business reviews and account-by-account triage; we don't both need to be in every conversation."
- "Your Sept 2025 priorities — deepen customer partnerships, scale AI-native offerings, expand the Americas footprint, strengthen partner ecosystem, build high-performance teams — read like the operating plan I just executed at Unify. The work I want to do next is exactly that, at Searce's scale and with Searce's partner equity in Google Cloud."
- "I'll lead with customer empathy because that's the only thing that actually compounds. The other stuff — architectures, IP economics, partner motion — follows from listening hard to the customer first. That's the EVLOS philosophy, and it's how I've operated for fifteen years."


## What "Why you" should land on
- A pattern of building governed agentic systems where measurement intelligence and platform reuse matter.
- Operating-model fluency across data science, product, platform, and global engineering.
- Honesty about uncertainty — willing to translate a credible interval rather than fake precision.

## Anti-patterns
- Do not list every prior role. Name two or three with a clear thread.
- Do not over-explain why you are leaving the current role.
- Do not promise transformation. Name the first 90 days.
